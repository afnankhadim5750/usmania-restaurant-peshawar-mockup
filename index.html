02 · The Room

04 · The Platter Board

Platters, built for sharing.

The review that comes up most often is about the platters: a full table, a little of everything, passed around until it is gone. Karahi, kebabs, naan and rice — served family-style, in the middle of the table.

05 · White Karahi

The white karahi regulars ask for.

Ask reviewers what to order and the white karahi comes up first — creamy where the usual karahi is tomato-sharp, mild in heat, finished with green chillies and fresh coriander. It is the dish that brings people back to this branch.

It arrives in the iron karahi it was cooked in, with Roghni naan to tear and share. Order it with a plate of kebabs and the table is set.

Some reviewers note the karahi can be uneven from visit to visit — the kitchen’s regulars say to ask for it the way you like it.

06 · From the Coals

Kebabs, off the charcoal.

Seekh kebab, chicken tikka and chapli kebab — grilled over charcoal and served with naan and salad. The kebabs are the other half of the Usmania order, and the part reviewers praise most often after the karahi.

07 · Naan & the Tandoor

Roghni naan, from the tandoor.

The bread is not an afterthought here — reviewers name the Roghni naan alongside the karahi. Seed-topped, blistered from the tandoor, it is what the karahi is eaten with.

08 · The Wedding Hall

One roof, for the big days too.

Usmania is a family restaurant with a wedding hall — the same kitchen, a bigger room. Walima dinners, family gatherings and wedding functions run here alongside the everyday restaurant, which is why so many Peshawar families know the hall as well as the dining room.

Planning a function? Call +92 91 5854846 to ask about dates and arrangements.

09 · Family Dining

Built for groups, not tables for two.

Usmania is a family restaurant in the plain sense: the room is set up for groups, the food is served to share, and the bill is for the table, not the plate. Families come from across Tehkal and University Town, and the wedding-hall crowd keeps the kitchen busy on weekends.

Come hungry, come with people, and order for the table — a karahi, a kebab platter, a stack of Roghni naan, and rice to finish.
